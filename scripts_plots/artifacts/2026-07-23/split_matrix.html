Reading the breakdown: each sub-number counts every leaking mutation once at its closest surviving match (mutually exclusive) — so they sum to the headline, and are not overlapping "does a relative exist" exposures.
What each split buys is the drop in the big number: by-complex removes the same-complex tier (matches RDE) but barely dents homology; clustered (CD-HIT ≤60% seq-id) removes the seq-homolog tier (the big win — this is MuLAN's retrain split); a CATH split removes the remaining fold-family tier (~6–28%, distant homologs below 60% id) to reach 0.

Notes. The retrain clustering is CD-HIT / mmseqs at ≥60% sequence identity / 0.8 cov (ProtBFF's criterion), complexes single-linked if they share any chain-cluster — this is the tier carrying the this-work numbers (clust 0.250, etc.). The CATH tier uses the both-partners superfamily rule (either-side single-linkage collapses the set) and is a ~70%-coverage lower bound, antibody-Ig-fold sensitive. The dashed iDist interface-dedup row is a leakage diagnostic, not a split — PPIRef/PPIformer's near-duplicate-only interface clustering at ≤0.03 (a different operation from the sequence-identity clustering, despite the shared word "cluster"). It removes duplicate interfaces only; that barely reduces leakage below by-complex (S1102 exact 87.5 → 80.2%), which is why the iDist benchmark is still homology-leaky, and MuLAN does not train on it. Near-dup clusters were on hand for S1102 only, so its other cells read ≈ by-complex.
