2 · Pooled Pearson pitfalls

📊
Bulk domination
Most mutations are near-neutral. A model that predicts near-zero with the right sign captures the bulk and scores high PCC while missing the large-effect cases that matter.
🧬
Between-complex variance
Pooling ~300 complexes, most variance is between them. Capturing per-complex offsets ("I've seen this complex") gives high pooled r with zero within-complex skill.
📏
Calibration sensitivity
Pearson rewards linear fit & is swung by outliers. A ΔΔG tool needs to rank, not calibrate — Spearman is robust to the heavy tail.
Smaller numbers = the metric getting stricter, not the model getting worse.

3 · AUROC — sign-of-effect

📐
Definition
Area under the ROC curve. Give each mutation a binary label — here destabilizing (measured ΔΔG>0, weakens binding) vs stabilizing/neutral — and rank all mutations by the model's predicted ΔΔG. AUROC is the probability a randomly chosen destabilizing mutation is ranked above a randomly chosen non-destabilizing one. 0.5 = chance, 1.0 = perfect; it's threshold-free (integrates over every cutoff).
🧮
How we compute it
Label by the sign of measured ΔΔG, score by predicted ΔΔG, take the Mann-Whitney AUROC (our auroc_destab). This is the RDE / USP-ddG convention, so it lines up directly with the frontier's reported "AUROC". It scores direction — "will this mutation weaken or strengthen binding?" — not magnitude, and needs no per-complex sample-size threshold.
🎯
Why it matters
Robust where correlation isn't: no T≥10 cutoff (unlike per-structure Spearman) and not inflated by the near-zero bulk or between-complex spread (unlike pooled Pearson). It's the triage metric — which mutations to make vs avoid — and it falls less than the pooled correlation as the split tightens (0.890 leaky → 0.695 clustered).
MuLAN's standout result: on the strict CATH split MuLAN+FoldX AUROC 0.791 ranks 2nd (above CATH-ddG 0.781, just under USP-ddG 0.802) — while its per-structure Spearman (0.438) is only mid-pack (4th of eleven on that tier — below flex-ddG 0.454, above published FoldX 0.430). So the model calls sign-of-effect near the top even when its exact within-complex ranking is middling. Two caveats: AUROC lives in a narrow ~0.6–0.8 band, so read ordering, not gaps; and it judges direction only — a model can score high AUROC yet still mispredict the magnitude of large-effect mutations (the tail that RMSE and the error-vs-ΔΔG analysis catch).

4 · Other metrics we keep

Precision@k — of the top-k mutations the model flags, the fraction that are genuinely large-|ΔΔG| — the retrieval view of the design task, and no T≥10 threshold needed.
Pooled Pearson/Spearman — reported only to compare with methods that quote pooled correlations (e.g. ProtBFF); never the headline.
